# Pixel Office visual rules

These rules apply to every new screen and every change to an existing screen. Empty-chair and misaligned-focus-ring bugs must not return. Later rules explicitly supersede earlier scene dimensions and furniture layouts where indicated.

1. **Seated agents occupy their own chairs, close to their desks, with visible upper bodies.** Each agent has a separate, fixed left/right seat in its desk island. The character faces away: head, neck, shoulders and upper back remain above the backrest. Draw the seat and legs behind the character and the lower-back backrest in front. The original seated character box rises 52 scene pixels at the chair and may naturally overlap its own desk edge. Visible character height must be **at least 1.6 times head height**, even in the lowest frame of the two-scene-pixel (one source pixel) breathing motion. Long hair may cover part of the back, while clothing remains visible at shoulders and upper back. Preserve all six hairstyles, cap/bun, skin/hair/clothing colors and eight glasses/tie/headphone combinations from behind. Ties are on the front and invisible from behind; only glasses arms are visible. Subtle typing and breathing frames must maintain these proportions. The gap from the top of an empty chair's backrest to the desk's front edge is **at most three source pixels**, targeting two scene pixels. Derive source pixels from the desk's vertical image scale; drawer and leg bottoms are not the desktop front edge. Occupied chairs use the same close position, regardless of aspect ratio or occupancy. Occupied chairs must not also enter the empty-furniture draw list; standing up restores the empty chair. Unit tests cover both measurements, positions, unique assignments, standing up and safe paths. `test:visual` checks actual upper-body pixels, empty-chair drawing and every appearance/animation combination. Only overlap with the character's own desk and chair is allowed; other furniture remains an obstacle.
2. **Avoid meaningless overlaps.** Keep desks, walls, department signs and walking characters separate. Names and bubbles follow the layers and placement rules below. Natural overlap of a seated character with its own chair and reception lettering with its counter is intentional. Center names immediately below their owner's feet, or chair when seated; never move them far away to find space. Draw names above furniture, floor and characters, below bubbles and signs. Text is 12 screen pixels independent of zoom, with a shared-theme status dot on the left and “…” for long names. Colliding labels may move by at most one label height and must then show a thin connector. If moving makes ownership ambiguous or does not resolve the collision, keep the selected agent, then the hovered agent, in front; make the other label translucent. A label's center may deviate horizontally by at most six source pixels; its top edge stays within one label height below its owner, and it must never be closer to another character. “Show names” defaults on and is remembered; when off, selected, hovered and keyboard-focused agents still show names. Working agents always show their bubble: above the head by default, beside or below it when space is limited. A small tail identifies its owner. Bubbles may overlap desktops and monitors, but must not cover department signs, other characters or bubbles. Text is at least 11 screen pixels, never splits words, uses at most two lines and truncates with “…”; one complete word plus “…” is the last resort. Unit tests and actual browser box intersections verify this.
3. **Characters stay on walkable ground.** Pathfinding accounts for the whole character box and never crosses desks, walls or signs. Sitting is a special position belonging to the assigned seat. World/path tests and `test:browser` inspect actual movement frames.
4. **Focus rings align with visible edges.** Use a 2px ring inside the edge with `outline-offset: -2px`, allowing 2px measurement tolerance. The message input and send button share one container, wrapped by `:focus-within`. The inner textarea has no border or ring; the send button sits inside at bottom right. Browser checks focus visible interaction types with the keyboard and separately measure login, search, recipients, agent/department forms, tasks, notes and rules. Closed detail panels are not visible elements; opened states are captured separately.
5. **Text stays inside its box.** Wrap text, including uninterrupted long words in messages. Intentional single-line truncation requires both `text-overflow: ellipsis` and `overflow: hidden`; full agent/department names remain available in title information. Reject `scrollWidth > clientWidth` and, in non-scrolling boxes, `scrollHeight > clientHeight`. Input fields may scroll within user text without deleting it.
6. **No horizontal page scrolling; no vertical scrolling on the Office page.** Management screens and long forms may scroll vertically inside their own panels. Measure document and Office bounds at all four viewport sizes.
7. **Text is readable in both themes.** All application colors come from shared variables in `office/src/theme.css`. Text/background, muted text, selected rows, buttons, badges and status text require at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text and decoration require 3:1. Token tests cover all defined pairs; browser tests check the composited background under actual visible text. The canvas keeps its warm palette while surroundings, labels and controls follow the theme.
8. **Compare with saved screenshots.** Store login, Office, expanded view, agents, agent/appearance/desk forms, departments, tasks/notes, company rules, events, approval inbox/file diff, folder connections, usage, run records/transcripts, outputs/previews, memory notes and two Coming soon screens under `office/tests/__screenshots__/`: light/dark × 1920×1080, 1366×768, 1280×640 and 390×844. Also capture focus, hover, selected working/waiting/idle/inactive agents, typing, bubbles, narrow navigation and long names/messages. Keep 2× crops of chairs, focus and other relevant regions in the same directory.
9. **Separate sidebar navigation from chat lists.** Navigation has two groups with distinct icons for every item. Lists under small muted “Group chats” and “Direct chats” headings start expanded and scroll independently; navigation and bottom controls stay fixed. Collapsed headings show counts. The logo row uses only a plain sidebar icon; hide the logo when collapsed. Do not add decorative arrows to section headings or the theme button. The bottom theme icon opens the same three-choice menu at both sidebar widths.
10. **Message outcomes and file deliveries remain readable.** The no-greeter hint, connection/queue/running/replied/failed message states, queue count, Retry and Stop controls must fit narrow screens. Output cards include name, size, preview and download. Version mismatches and failed handoffs use shared error colors. Queued-agent indicators follow existing bubble-placement rules.
11. **Replace browser-default scrollbars; avoid abruptly clipped list items.** Use an 8px track, rounded 6px thumb and theme tokens, a transparent track and no arrow buttons. Firefox uses thin scrollbars and shared colors; Chromium/Safari use the same custom treatment. Reserve scrollbar space in chat and lists. Sidebar list heights align to whole rows; partially visible rows fade at scrolling edges. Headings, navigation and bottom controls stay fixed; expanded lists reserve at least three items on short desktop screens. `tests/chat.audit.ts` measures real scrollable areas: scrollbar size, arrow/track style, bubble spacing, list height and edge fading. Run it in `test:browser` and every `test:visual` capture, with long chat and 20 agents/10 departments at four sizes and both themes.
12. **Distinguish chat messages from events.** Errors, stops and system notices are muted event rows; merge consecutive events of the same kind and allow expanding details. Agent bubbles are left-aligned, owner bubbles right-aligned, at most 78% wide. Show name/portrait once per consecutive group; read/delivery and run status belong only to owner messages. Preserve day separators, safe Markdown and expandable messages after roughly 12 lines. The composer starts at one line and grows to eight, with the recipient label inside. Show a short no-greeter notice only when sending. New messages must not pull someone reading earlier messages to the bottom; a new-message button returns to the end. Browser tests check these behaviors and ensure HTML cannot execute; full screenshots and 2× crops use `a2-4-*` names.

13. **One fixed map with reserved seats.** B1.5 removed world redistribution based on window size or agent count. The B1.6 map has 176 × 108 tiles and a 73-tile work area. Twelve paired islands in six columns × two rows and 24 seats are drawn even when empty; beyond 24 agents, add rows using the same pattern. Department ownership and rug color may change; aspect ratio never moves furniture. Assign whole islands in department order. An odd-sized department leaves its paired seat empty and uses one rug and sign. Add rows of six islands beyond twelve. Unassigned desks have no rug/sign. Every named seat record includes chair, desk, screen, approach tile, seated position, direction and chair/character layers. A* uses only an open four-neighbor grid. Reserve body-covered tiles, the occupied tile and a started next step; each seat target has one owner. A seated agent reserves its actual seat and releases the approach tile. After waiting four seconds, return to the reserved tile and find a new path; without a path, stay in place. Employees cannot use the Director room for transit or breaks.

Active agents start at their seats. Inactive agents are not drawn: the chair is empty, screen off and desk label muted. Seated agents face desks: upward at work islands; opposite up/down directions at meetings; right/left at table ends; the Director faces the viewer. For rear-facing desks, redraw only the desk front over the body, preserving head and upper body. Do not also draw an empty chair under an occupied seat. Sitting/standing uses a special transition between approach point and owned seat. Normal walking cannot overlap other furniture.

Calm idle breaks occur every 4–8 minutes, lively breaks every 1–3 minutes, lasting 20–60 seconds. Limit breaks to a quarter of active agents, at least one, and the entrance water/sofa area. New work interrupts a break. Still or reduced-motion mode returns everyone to their seats and stops walking/seated motion while preserving monitor state. Handoffs use two opposing meeting seats; three-person chains and multiple mentions use the relevant seats. Larger groups safely stay at their desks; crowd meetings were not completed. “Call a meeting” supports at most three active agents; explain why it is disabled for larger offices and start no work. Timing starts once everyone sits: 10–20 seconds for handoffs, 30 for groups, then return to desks. Show the speaker's bubble in turn and turn listeners' heads toward the speaker. Server work is independent of walking.

14. **Simple rooms, monitors and the Director.** The original layout has four windows/two pictures on the upper wall, one printer/filing-cabinet group on the right and three plants. Work desks have two clear screens, keyboard and mouse, at most one small mug per seat. Signs, bubble strip, screen/desk, character and name occupy separate vertical zones. No kitchen, noticeboard, bin, coat stand, vending machine, foosball or random floating objects. Three lower rooms have continuous partitions and one clear top doorway each. The left room originally contains a large-screen executive desk, built-in Director in dark suit/white shirt/burgundy tie, two guest chairs, bookcase/picture and a rug with sofa/coffee table. This scene character is not an employee-list entry. Screen and character emit the same `office:director-screen` mouse/keyboard event; hovering highlights the screen. Entrance contains counter, sofa, cooler and plant. Meeting room contains a long wood-textured table with border, papers and laptop, a presentation screen and 12 seats by default, up to 24. Extend the table beyond twelve; chairs match work-chair scale. Lower-room widths are roughly 30% Director, 20% entrance, 50% meeting.

Working monitors show scrolling lines, idle monitors a calm lit screen, waiting monitors a question, and blocked/error job descriptions a warning color. Empty seats and agents away from desks have dark screens. Typing/breathing phases depend on agent identity; idle head turns and a short completion motion are included. A 10–15-minute Director walk, stretching, coffee and phone/notebook frames were not implemented; do not draw broken partial frames.

Expanded view preserves B1.4 floating chat and drafts. Scale/pan the same map on narrow screens; phone starting zoom is at least 0.30× for readable text. Department plaques, labels and bubbles stay readable in screen pixels. Normal desktop height follows map aspect ratio; Fit at widths 1920 and 1366 leaves at most eight screen pixels at edges. Expanded view may have symmetric theme-colored space depending on aspect ratio; never stretch the world. Map/walking/seating/monitor/meeting tests and `browser.b15.ts` cover four sizes × two themes × 4/14/24 agents. ## Checks and comparison

From `office`:

```sh
npm run typecheck
npm test
npm run build
npm run test:browser
npm run test:visual
```

`test:visual` opens no listening port: Chromium receives the built app through Fastify injection. Only the simulated `http://ofis.test` origin is allowed. Accounts, tasks and notes are fixtures; no real Tududi account is created. Time, randomness and render time are fixed; reduced motion stabilizes capture transitions. Live movement checks belong to `test:browser`.

After changing focus, require two consecutive identical full screenshots before accepting a capture. This prevents incomplete text rendering from entering comparison; a screen that fails to stabilize fails the test.

A3/B1 checks request a complete repaint after scrolling/focus by shifting the root view one pixel and restoring it; capture preserves position/focus. Clip the decorative logo icon to its cell and hide it from screen readers. Pixel thresholds remain unchanged.

A2.4 removes Chromium's default test option that hides scrollbars. Full captures and 2× crops include real thin scrollbars and measure actual reserved width, beyond CSS declarations.

Normal tests **never change baselines**. Write new captures and error/diff files under `office/.test-data/visual/`. `karsilastirma.json` reports changed pixels per image. **One pixel with a channel difference above 16 fails**; smaller channel differences tolerate antialiasing. This threshold follows an observed 13/255 difference at one edge pixel of the same diagonal status icon. There is no changed-pixel-count allowance. Changed dimensions or missing baselines also fail.

For an intentional design change, inspect full captures and 2× crops before explicitly refreshing:

```sh
npm run test:visual -- --update-baselines
npm run test:visual
```

`taban.json` records browser version and image list. Review differences after browser/font changes. Use `VISUAL_FILTER=1366x768-aydinlik npm run test:visual` only for investigation; delivery comparison runs unfiltered. Tests deliberately introduce incorrect chair lists, shifted rings, low contrast and unexpected image marks to prove detection.

## Limits

Box measurements and sampled frames are not mathematical proof of every possible animation. Image details in the pixel palette are outside text-token contrast tests. Closed or hidden elements are measured only in their captured open states.

B2.4 replaces the old Director message form with a real terminal; old form/input focus measurements no longer apply. Desktop/phone captures under `.test-data/b2-browser/` are review artifacts, not stable pixel baselines. Full `test:browser`, `test:visual` and baseline refresh remain necessary; terminal changes did not refresh old baselines. Unsafe meeting motion for four or more people remains disabled and appears as **Large meeting · coming soon**. The Director's short walk is also unimplemented.
